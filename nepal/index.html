04 · CULTURE

The festival calendar

Nepal's working week is Sunday to Friday and its year is held together by festivals. Timing a visit around one changes the whole trip.

01

Dashain

15 days, Sep–Oct (Shrawan–Ashwin)

The longest and biggest Hindu festival of the year, honouring Durga's victory. Ghatasthapana sows the sacred jamara; Fulpati brings the deity in; on Vijayadashami elders put tika and bless, and the country eats, flies kites, swings on bamboo ping, and reconciles.

Nationwide
02

Tihar / Deepawali

5 days, Oct–Nov (Kartik)

The festival of lights and of debts owed to animals: crows, then dogs, then cows are worshipped, Laxmi is invited in at dusk on the third night, and brothers and sisters exchange bhai tika on the fifth. Every windowsill holds a diyo.

Nationwide
03

Holi / Fagu Purnima

Feb–Mar (Falgun full moon)

Colour, water and the burning of the symbolic Hiraka effigy the night before. In Patan crowds gather around a pole on a plaza that has been assembled from a single tree trunk.

Nationwide; Patan's Tingun Chher is the spectacle
04

Chhath

4 days after Tihar, Oct–Nov

The most austere and beautiful rite in Nepal: devotees fast without water, then stand waist-deep in cold river water at dawn and dusk to offer arghya to the rising and setting sun, for their children's lives.

Terai — Janakpur, Dhanusha, Siraha
05

Indra Jatra

4 days, Sep (Bhadra)

The city's greatest street festival. A wooden chariot is pulled through lanes, masked Lakhe and Pulukisi dance, the great bhairav drums sound all night, and the living goddess Kumari is carried in her ratha to bless the city.

Kathmandu
06

Losar

Feb–Mar, Tibetan lunar new year

Tibetan new year: guthuk noodle soup on the eve, butter tea, cham mask dances in the gompa, and homes scrubbed and re-whitewashed. In Khumbu it rivals Dashain in feeling.

Sherpa, Tamang and Dolpo villages
07

Maghe Sankranti / Maghi

14–15 Jan (Magh 1)

Sun enters Capricorn; winter breaks. Families eat ghee, til and sweet potato, take ritual dips at Devighat and Triveni, and Tharu communities dance, wrestle and drink the year's first rice beer.

Nationwide; Tharu Maghi in the Terai
08

Sakela (Sungwa / Tawang)

Jan (full moon) and Jun (new moon)

Kirat Mundhum thanksgiving for the harvest, danced in concentric rings for days on hillsides — arguably the oldest continuous festival calendar in the Himalaya.

Rai and Limbu villages, east
09

Biska Jatra / Bhandu Plate Festival

9 days around Apr (Baisakh)

The year turns in Bhaktapur: a linga of wood dragged to the temple, oil lamps on a plate thrown to the crowd, and the village deities carried out of the gates on the final day, and devotees thrown beneath the chariot wheels at the Bhandu plate rite.

Bhaktapur
10

Rato Machhindranath Jatra

Apr–Jun, weeks-long

The longest chariot festival in Asia, pulling the red lord of rain and harvest through Patan's lanes to guarantee the monsoon and the valley's rice.

Patan
05 · PEOPLES

Over a hundred languages, one valley of makers

The groups that made Nepal what a visitor actually meets.

Newar

Kathmandu Valley

The valley's builders and artists. Their cities, metalwork, woodcarving, brick-and-slate houses, and the Guthi communal trust system that has funded festivals for six centuries make Nepali civilisation what the world recognises as it.

Sherpa

Khumbu, Solu, Rolwaling

Nepali for 'eastern people'. Highland adaptation, Nyingma Buddhism, and the mountaineering skill that made every commercial Himalayan expedition possible.

Tharu

Terai belt

Malaria-resistant people of the malarial Terai forest, with their own language, house-raising murals, the stick dance and a cuisine of river fish and forest greens.

Gurung & Magar

Gandaki, Lumbini hills

Hill farmers and the historic recruiting heartland of the Gurkhas, whose tamang sass and kaudi dancing, and whose bride-price songs, structure the whole festival year.

Kirat — Rai & Limbu

Eastern hills

The oldest continuously inhabited culture of Nepal, with the Mundhum scripture, sung oral history, and a shamanic tradition still performed by the Jhankri.

Maithili / Mithila

Terai east

A classical literary culture with its own script tradition, Madhubani painting made by women on walls and paper, and Vidyapati's verses still sung at weddings.

Tamang

Himal rim, Langtang, Rasuwa

The most numerous Tibetan-origin group in Nepal, whose hyama horse dancers and Bomchi and Lama ritual tradition dominate the northern valleys around Kathmandu.

Living faith

Everywhere

Hindu and Buddhist practice overlap so completely that the same priest may serve both, the same Newar family keeps both calendars, and a stupa and a temple frequently share a courtyard.

06 · KITCHEN

What you will actually eat

Hill fermentation, Terai river fish, Newar festival platters, and dal bhat that refills itself.

Dal Bhatदाल भात

The national meal and the trekker's engine: lentil soup, rice, tarkari, achar, and — on a good day — more refills than you can accept. 'Dal bhat power, 24 hour' is true.

Momoमोमो

Steamed or jhol-filled dumplings of buff, chicken or vegetables, with a tomato-sesame achar that defines an evening anywhere in Nepal.

ILLUSTRATED

Fermented leaf greens, sun-dried and stored — the winter protein and umami of hill pantries, eaten as achar or soup, and now studied as traditional preservation science.

Millet distillate and millet beer: tongba served in a bamboo vessel with hot water and a straw, passed around and refilled, is the ritual of Limbu and Tamang hospitality.

ILLUSTRATED

Buff or chicken slow-cooked over a coal flame with timur and mustard oil, the sound and smell of a Nepali winter evening.

ILLUSTRATED

ILLUSTRATED

Eastern hill gardens grow single-estate orthodox tea that won international awards; the same hills produce Gulmi and western coffee now finding world buyers.

07 · WILDLIFE

The Terai is the surprise

Most visitors come for mountains and leave having never seen a rhino. The southern grass belt holds some of the subcontinent's better conservation stories.

Greater one-horned rhino

Vulnerable

Reduced to a few hundred by poaching and habitat loss, then brought back by community anti-poaching work to hold most of the world's remaining population. Chitwan's are habituated to canoe traffic.

Chitwan, Bardia, Suklaphanta, Koshi

Bengal tiger

Endangered

Nepal doubled its wild tiger estimate in roughly a decade. Bardia, not Chitwan, is where a sighting is likeliest — on a boat on the Karnali, not a jeep on a road.

Chitwan, Bardia, Suklaphanta

Asian elephant

Endangered

Wild herds move between the Terai and the hills. The captive population at the Elephant Breeding Centre is a conservation breeding programme; tourism that rides working elephants is best avoided.

Chitwan, Bardia, Shuklaphanta

Gharial & marsh mugger

Critically endangered (gharial)

Long-snouted fish-eating crocodiles on sandbanks. Hatched and released from the Gharial Rehabilitation Centre at Kasara on the Narayani — the canoe trip is also the survey.

Narayani, Karnali, Koshi

Gangetic river dolphin

Endangered

Blind-navigating freshwater dolphins in one of the few major Himalayan rivers still largely free of dams. Boat at dawn on the lower Karnali.

Karnali at Bardia

Snow leopard

Vulnerable

The high desert's ghost. Community-managed trails in Dolpo now run tracked camera surveys; seeing one is luck, hearing about one is common.

Dolpo, Manang, Humla

Himalayan birds

900+ species recorded

From the satyr tragopan and the danphe, the Impeyan pheasant that is the national bird — Nepal sits on several major Central Asian flyways and a bird list longer than most European countries.

Koshi Tappu, Annapurna, Langtang

Swamp deer (barasingha)

Endangered

Suklaphanta holds the world's last significant herd of barasingha, grazing open grassland at sunset in numbers no other site can show.

Suklaphanta

09 · WHEN

Four ways to visit

BEST

Autumn — Oct & Nov

Clear, dry, cold nights at altitude

Post-monsoon air is at its cleanest; the mountains stand up sharply and the festival calendar is at full volume with Dashain and Tihar back to back. Trails are busiest; book teahouses and guides well ahead.

EXCELLENT

Spring — Mar to May

Warm valleys, hazy afternoons

Rhododendron in full colour up to 3,000 m, lower passes clear of snow, and the climbing season in full swing at base camp. Afternoon cloud builds earlier in the day than in autumn.

GOOD, SELECTIVELY

Winter — Dec to Feb

Cold high, mild low

Wonderful below 2,000 m and in the Terai parks — rhino and tiger viewing is at its best. High passes are snowbound and Kathmandu nights are genuinely cold with no central heating anywhere.

USE THE RAIN SHADOW

Monsoon — Jun to Sep

Heavy west-to-east rain

The main reason to come in the monsoon is to go where it does not reach: Upper Mustang, Dolpo, and the Trans-Himalayan rain shadow. Elsewhere expect wet trails, leeches, landslides and cancelled mountain flights.

10 · GUIDELINES

Permits, courtesy, safety and the practical

Fees, permits and border rules change. Figures here were checked against official Nepali sources on the date above — confirm with the Department of Immigration, NTAB or a registered trekking agency before travel.

Licensed guide is now the rule

Most trekking regions

Nepal's mandatory-guide policy requires permits to be obtained through a registered trekking agency with a certified guide on the trek. Independent trekkers in many regions must engage one; solo trekking on classified routes is not permitted.

TIMS has largely receded

Route-dependent

The Trekkers' Information Management System has been dropped or superseded in the Khumbu and Annapurna regions, replaced by local-body fees and conservation-area permits. Some routes still require it. Carry your agency's documents regardless.

Restricted Area Permit (RAP)

Trans-Himalayan zones

Required for Upper Mustang, Dolpo, Manaslu, Kanchenjunga, Tsum Valley, Nar Phu, and parts of Humla. Only a registered agency can obtain it, a licensed guide is mandatory, group minimums apply (commonly two people), and it is priced per day, per zone, in US dollars.

Conservation area entry — ACAP, MCAP, KCA

Gandaki, Manaslu, east

Annapurna, Manaslu and Kanchenjunga conservation areas each issue their own entry permit, obtainable in Kathmandu or Pokhara with passport photos and your agency paperwork. Fees fund the teahouse trail and community projects.

National park & rural municipality fees

All parks

Sagarmatha National Park plus the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu rural municipality fee for Everest country; Langtang, Chitwan, Bardia, Shey Phoksundo, Rara and Koshi Tappu each charge separately. Most are paid at the gate, in cash, and change is scarce.

Lukla flights and weather

Khumbu

The STOL runway at Tenzing-Hillary Airport closes in poor visibility, and cancellations are common — budget two or three spare days either side of a Lukla return, or plan the Jiri route on foot.

  • 01

    Walk clockwise around stupas, chortens and mani walls — the kora goes right, always right.

  • 02

    Remove shoes at every temple threshold, and at the door of a home. Your socks may wear through; that is accepted.

  • 03

    Never point the soles of your feet at a person, an altar, or a book of scripture.

  • 04

    Ask before photographing people, and never photograph a ritual object, a cremation, or a puja without explicit permission.

  • 05

    Use the right hand for giving, receiving, eating and money. The left hand is reserved for bodily use.

  • 06

    Do not touch the head of an adult, or step over food, books, or musical instruments.

  • 07

    Accept tsampa, tongba, or raksi with both hands and at least taste it; refusal of hospitality is remembered.

  • 08

    Dress modestly in towns: shoulders and knees covered. On treks this relaxes only above the tree line.

  • 09

    Say Namaste with palms together rather than reaching to shake hands, especially with elders and monks.

  • 10

    In monasteries, never sit on a lama's throne, handle ritual objects, or turn a prayer wheel the wrong way.

01

Acute mountain sickness is the real danger

Above roughly 2,500 m ascend no more than 300–500 m per sleeping altitude, hydrate, and recognise headache, nausea and breathlessness at rest early. Descent is the treatment; no summit is worth your brain.

02

Travel insurance must cover helicopter evacuation

Standard policies often exclude trekking above a set altitude or exclude heli rescue. Confirm the ceiling, the altitude limit and that Nepal is covered, and carry the policy number on your person.

03

Only registered operators on the mountains

Check the agency against the government list before paying. Untreated porters, missing insurance for staff, and unlicensed guides are the common failures; a cheap quote usually means someone is being underpaid.

04

Roads are the biggest risk, not peaks

Mountain highways are narrow, single-lane, and share space with overloaded trucks; night bus travel is best avoided and micro-bus journeys take far longer than the map suggests.

05

Water and food hygiene

Drink sealed or treated water, avoid ice outside reputable kitchens, and peel raw produce. On treks, buy boiled or filtered water and refuse plastic bottles you cannot dispose of.

06

Monsoon hazards

Landslides close the Prithvi and BP highways without notice, rivers rise within an hour, and flights to Lukla cancel for days. Keep float time in the schedule in Jun–Sep.

07

Air quality and Kathmandu

Valley inversion traps winter particulates; dust and vehicle emissions make Nov–Feb the roughest months for lungs. Carry masks if you are sensitive and plan indoor time midday.

08

Communication and power

Ncell and Nepal Telecom both cover the trails unevenly; download offline maps, carry a power bank (teahouses charge for electricity above ~3,000 m), and register your itinerary with your embassy and your agency.

VISA & ENTRY
  • Tourist visa is issued on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport and at designated land crossings, and can also be obtained at Nepali missions abroad.
  • Multiple-entry fees: US$30 for 15 days, US$50 for 30 days, US$125 for 90 days, payable in convertible foreign currency.
  • Total stay may not exceed 150 days in a single Nepali calendar year. Fees are non-refundable and card payment at the airport counters has historically been unreliable.
  • Fill the online arrival form before you fly to skip the queue, and carry one passport photo — the kiosk printers are not guaranteed.
MONEY
  • Currency is the Nepali rupee (NPR), subdivided into paisa. Indian rupee notes are not legal tender for ordinary purchases in Nepal.
  • Cards work in Kathmandu, Pokhara and larger hotels; remote teahouses run entirely on cash. ATMs are unreliable on the trails, so draw in the city.
  • Budget the small notes: teahouse owners rarely change a 1,000-rupee note for a 100-rupee coffee.
GETTING AROUND
  • Kathmandu connects to Pokhara by tourist bus, by a short domestic flight, or increasingly by the new corridor; mountain roads take 7–10 hours when the schedule says 6.
  • Domestic flights (Tara Air, Yeti/Summit, Shree) are the practical way to reach Lukla, Jumla, Nepalganj and Bharatpur — and the first thing weather cancels.
  • Local transport in the valley is by electric-tempo three-wheelers, metered taxis, and the Kathmandu Durbar Square–bound routes; app ride-hailing works in the cities.
ESSENTIALS
  • Time zone is UTC+05:45 — a deliberate offset. Set your watch twice; most phone calendars handle it correctly but hotel desks still quote 'Nepali time' loosely.
  • Country code +977. Mains are 230 V at 50 Hz with round-pin Type C/D/M plugs; bring an adapter and a surge-tolerant charger.
  • The greeting is Namaste; the working week is Sunday to Friday, so Saturday is the day everything is closed and everything is out.
  • Tip guiding staff on the terms agreed, not on the market rate at home: a guide and a porter are the difference between a trip and a rescue.
