Designing a Research Question That Survives Review

समीक्षा टिक्ने अनुसन्धान प्रश्न कसरी बनाउने

ACTTOLOG EDITORIAL·17 Sept 2026·1 MIN READ

Most rejected proposals fail in the same place: the research question. Not because the topic is uninteresting, but because the question is unanswerable, unmeasurable, or already answered.

Topic, problem, question

A topic is a subject area. A problem is a tension inside that area — something unresolved, contradictory or unmeasured. A question is a specific, testable formulation of that tension.

  • Topic: remote work and productivity.
  • Problem: studies disagree on the direction and size of the effect.
  • Question: under which task types does remote work change measured output for knowledge workers?

Three filters

  1. Reachability — can you obtain the data, sample or documents within your timeframe?
  2. Measurability — is there a defensible operational definition for each construct?
  3. Contribution — if answered, does something change for a reader, a policy or a field?
A question that passes two filters is a chapter. A question that passes three is a thesis.

Then reverse-engineer the method

Once the question is fixed, the methodology writes itself: the design follows the question type, the sample follows the population, the analysis follows the construct definitions. That is the order reviewers expect.

IN THIS ARTICLE

A research question is not a topic. It is a claim about what is unknown, why it matters, and how it can be answered with the data you can actually reach.
